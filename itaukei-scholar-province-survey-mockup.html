For the past two years, this has been my passion — a side project I have spent much of my time and money on. I have been quietly curating a database of iTaukei scholarly work — theses, journal articles, books, chapters, reports — and sharing it in a closed Facebook group for iTaukei scholars and iTaukei at large.

Part of the reason I do this is that it is not iTaukei / Pacific island culture to talk about our own achievements — successfully finishing graduate studies, publishing a thesis, a journal article, a book. Sharing on someone else's behalf takes that pressure off the scholar, and lets the rest of us actually see the depth of their work.

The Facebook group only reaches its members, though, and each post is a one-off — it does not stay accessible to iTaukei outside the group, to iTaukei communities, to other iTaukei scholars, or to anyone else who would benefit from knowing about iTaukei research. So I built a public database and dashboard: publications in and on Fiji, by iTaukei scholars and others, including publications by iTaukei scholars living outside Fiji. Same purpose — to showcase and elevate our scholarly work, and to make it accessible to the wider public, especially to decision-makers.

Every iTaukei scholar with two or more publications — thesis, journal article, book, book chapter, or report — gets their own profile in the database (see a sample). The dashboard is searchable, filters live, and each scholar has an AI-written plain-language summary plus diverse auto-generated keywords so you can see the breadth of their work at a glance.

Why I'm asking for your help. One of the most useful things the dashboard does is let you drill down by confederacy and paternal province. That drilldown only works if we actually know each scholar's village and province — and for many, we still do not. If you know a scholar's village and province — paternal side, maternal side, or both — please add what you know below. You can also add scholars who are not in the database yet.
